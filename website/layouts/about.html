What is {{ $city.siteName }}?

{{ $city.about.intro | safeHTML }}

Every night at midnight {{ $city.cityName }} time, the site reads {{ $roundedSources }} public calendars, feeds, and APIs (see the sources), merges events that more than one source lists, and drops events that are over. Every listing links to the organizer's own page, which has the full details and the final word on times, tickets, and changes.

The site stores only what the list needs: title, times, venue, city, category, and a short description. It keeps no ticket prices, photos, or contact details, and it never works around a site that asks not to be read.

Size

An estimate of the crowd, not a count or a capacity. It is a guess, worked out from the first of these that applies:

Cost

The cheapest adult ticket at face value, before fees, sorted into a tier. Only the tier is kept, never the price. It comes from the source's own price data, or from a price written in the event's description. Kids, senior, student, and member prices are ignored; for a range, the low end counts. A suggested donation, or free entry with paid parking or food, is Free.

Category

Each event has one category, from its source (a theater's events are Performing Arts) or, for sources that mix many kinds, from words in its title and the source's own labels. A holiday concert is Holiday and Seasonal first.

The map

Map shows the events that match your current search and filters. Drag to move around, and pinch or scroll to zoom. Nearby events are grouped into a circle with a count; tap it to zoom in, or tap a pin to see what's happening there.

A filled pin is the venue itself. Its location comes from the event's own listing, or from looking the venue up on OpenStreetMap. An outlined pin means we only know the town or ZIP code, so the pin sits at its center. Estate sales and auctions are always shown this way, because {{ $city.siteName }} never stores a home's address.

If you allow it, the map opens where you are. Your location stays in your browser: {{ $city.siteName }} never sends it, stores it, or logs it. If you say no, or you're outside the area, the map shows the whole region instead.

Meetup events

Meetup group events are hidden until you check Include Meetups, because many groups are only partly public. The events are posted publicly, but some groups let only members RSVP or see the exact location. You may need a free Meetup account, and to join the group, before you can go.

Library events

Library events are hidden until you check Include Libraries, because the libraries list so many programs (story times, crafts, clubs, and classes) that they would crowd out everything else. An event counts as a library event when it is held at a library or listed on a library's own calendar. Searching for "library" shows them without checking the box.
